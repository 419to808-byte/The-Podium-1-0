The Verbatim Rule

By the rules of this portal, the highest-voted post MUST be read aloud — word for word, exactly as its author wrote it — and discussed, by a representative of the United States, in front of the entire country. Another post, drawn at random, must be read and discussed the same way.

AI will be used to detect profanity and errors, correct grammar, and suggest clearer formatting. It will never steer you: your topic, your position, or your meaning. And once your post is published, it is final.

Keep in mind that your post may be read in front of the entire nation. Write in your most professional manner. Profanity, slurs, and personal attacks will not post. State your idea or concern plainly and professionally. You are not posting a comment. You are addressing the nation — speaking to and for every American. Treat it with the respect it deserves.

Fine print (official register)

All submissions are subject to automated pre-publication review. Submissions containing profanity, slurs, or personal attacks shall not be published. Submissions containing slang or errors materially affecting comprehensibility shall be held pending correction by the submitting party, with suggested revisions provided for consideration; no submission shall be altered by the System without the submitting party’s express action. Weekly selections shall consist of (i) the submission receiving the highest number of votes during the applicable period and (ii) one (1) submission selected by random drawing from among all eligible published submissions. Selected submissions shall be read aloud in their entirety, verbatim, and discussed in the weekly briefing. A published submission is final and shall not be edited or redacted.

Statements of policy, principles, and fundamentals

The purpose of this platform is to ensure that every citizen has the opportunity to have their concerns or ideas heard by the nation. Citizens will address the nation and speak on its behalf.

  • The highest-voted post and one randomly drawn post are read aloud word-for-word, in full, by a U.S. representative and discussed in the weekly briefing. No editor stands between author and nation. Nothing is edited after posting.
  • AI detects problems and suggests wording before publication only. It never steers a post's topic, position, or meaning, and never changes, substitutes, or declines a post on its own.
  • Profanity, slurs, and personal attacks are hard-blocked and never publish. Slang, misspellings, grammar, and unclear text are held with suggested rewording; the author accepts or retypes, and AI never silently changes text. Held drafts are not published, do not enter the draw, and do not use the citizen's one-post slot.
  • Once published, a post is final. It cannot be deleted, withdrawn, archived, or hidden.
  • Voting is upvote-only. One vote per citizen per weekly cycle, switchable until voting closes Sunday at 11:59 PM ET.
  • One post per citizen per weekly cycle.
  • Posts are capped at 630 words.
  • The random draw is rig-proof: the eligible pool is frozen in a fixed published order at the cutoff, and the position is the NIST Randomness Beacon value mod pool size, plus one. The draw is internal only, with no button, replay, or re-roll.
  • The committee prepares answers only. It never alters, substitutes, delays, or declines a selection. It reviews selections a week ahead and routes each answer to the correct branch and level.
  • The committee does not fund. It asks for funds and allocates funds.
  • Committee answers start as AI drafts, reviewed, edited, and approved by a real committee member who is the answerer of record. AI never delivers a final answer alone and never touches a citizen post. The public sees only approved answers.
  • An exact-duplicate post in the same cycle does not create a new post; the citizen is offered an upvote on the existing post, with no post-anyway exit. The draw pool excludes the winner and any post exactly matching the winner. Similar-but-distinct posts are advisory only.
  • The Live Feed shows the current cycle only. Past-cycle posts that were never selected are deleted; selected posts are kept as the briefing record.
  • Posting and voting run Monday 12:00 AM to Sunday 11:59 PM ET. The pool freezes at the Sunday cutoff, the draw is Monday at 12:00 PM ET, the committee has the week plus a Monday-Tuesday buffer, and the briefing airs Wednesdays at 1:00 PM ET as the fixed opening segment.
  • This Week shows only the most recently aired briefing. Next Week shows the next briefing's selections. A briefing's replay publishes when that briefing airs. Prior Posts & Answers and Status Updates list aired selections only.
  • Language: a citizen may post in any language. The original post is the official record and is read aloud verbatim in the author's language. Translations are machine aids for readers and never replace the original.
  • Security: every post carries a SHA-256 hash, and every action is recorded in a hash-chained audit log.

            Committee

            The committee is 12 ordinary verified citizens, chosen by random draw, serving 12-week terms, with one new citizen joining every week. The committee reviews and approves answers, follows through until the issue is resolved, and does not fund — it asks for and allocates funds.

            Committee rules — selection, pay, structure & schedule

            How members are selected

            Twelve ordinary verified citizens, chosen by random draw from a national pool using the NIST Randomness Beacon — the same rig-proof method as the post draw. Selection is a summons, not a draft: if your name comes up, you agree to serve or decline and the next citizen is drawn. To serve you must be a verified citizen, 18 or older, with no post in the current cycle, not a government employee, and not currently incarcerated or on parole or probation — a past felony does not exclude you; eligibility returns 5 years after the sentence is fully complete. Terms are 12 weeks, one new citizen joins every week, and after cycling out you sit out 12 weeks. No more than 2 terms per year.

            Pay

            Members are paid $150 per day, once a week, for the Wednesday vote. Travel, hotel, and meals for the weekly in-person meeting are covered separately at government rates and booked directly — members never pay out of pocket — plus childcare support for in-person days and an optional top-up for shift workers who can show they lose more than the base stipend.

            Structure

            Twelve members, each holding an equal share of the decision-making power. Government agency liaisons advise only and do not vote. Each week the committee receives the selected posts and the AI-drafted answers and votes on whether to follow the AI suggestions. A split vote means no action on that post this week and the post reverts to shine light — it is still read aloud and tracked, never buried. The narrator and the committee spokesperson are permanent salaried roles who present only: the narrator reads posts word-for-word and the spokesperson presents the committee's approved answers. Neither has any authority over posts or answers. If either quits or is fired, a selection, election, or hiring process replaces them.

            Weekly schedule

            Members receive the selected posts as soon as they are selected and have 6 days on their own to think about them. They all meet in person on Wednesday morning to deliberate and vote. Then the narrator and the committee spokesperson make the script and record live at 1:00 PM ET. The Podium funds a round-trip ticket and a hotel for every member every week.

            Starting the program: the first committee

            The program starts with 12 new citizens selected at once, by the same NIST draw and summons as every later selection.

            To reach the steady rotation — one member joining and one leaving each Monday — the first 12 serve staggered starting terms. Each member is assigned an exit week, 1 through 12, by a single NIST random draw at the first meeting. The member assigned week 1 cycles out after one week of service; the member assigned week 2 cycles out after two weeks; and so on until the member assigned week 12, who serves the full twelve weeks.

            Each Monday, the member cycling out is replaced by a new member drawn the same way, so the committee always has 12 serving members. After the twelfth week, every seat is on a normal 12-week term and the steady rotation is in place.

            A full 12-week term pays $300 a week — $3,600. The member who cycles out first serves one week and is paid $300: the biggest financial loss of the starting group, $3,300 less than a full term. Each later exit loses less; the member who serves all twelve weeks loses nothing.

            This is not a penalty. The staggered exit is what makes the rotation possible: without it, all 12 would leave at once after twelve weeks and the committee would lose all of its experience in a single week. With it, eleven experienced members always remain to carry the work and teach the newcomer. The first members out are the ones who make the program work — their short first terms are a crucial part of starting it, not a punishment for anything they did.

            A short starting term still counts as a term for the sit-out rule: after cycling out, a member sits out twelve weeks before being eligible again.

              The Process

              1. A citizen posts an idea or concern — 630 words max, gate-checked, final once published. 2. The community votes — each citizen casts one vote per weekly cycle, switchable until voting closes Sunday at 11:59 PM ET. 3. At the cutoff the pool freezes; Monday at 12:00 PM ET the NIST Randomness Beacon draw picks one post at random. 4. The committee prepares answers and routes each selection to the correct branch and level. 5. Wednesday at 1:00 PM ET the briefing airs — the highest-voted post and the drawn post are read aloud word-for-word and discussed. 6. Approved answers are published, and follow-through is tracked in Status Updates until Answered, Action Promised, In Progress, Resolved — or No Action, with an official reason.

              Security

              Every post carries a SHA-256 hash, and every action is recorded in a hash-chained audit log. The real product is planned to verify identity through Login.gov and use phishing-resistant sign-in, designed to meet federal security standards. The backend items are not yet built.
